# NeverQuest for WoW Forever

By NeverHeal84.

A leveling guide, raid attunement guides, route planner, quest tracker, waypoint arrow, spawn and objective maps, quest buttons and XP tracker in one addon, built for the WoW Forever client (interface 16001).

## Install

1. Close the game.
2. Unzip so you have a folder named `NeverQuest` (it holds `NeverQuest_Camelot.toc` and the `.lua` files).
3. Put that folder in your Forever client's `Interface\AddOns` folder, next to any other Forever addons you use. If you had an earlier NeverQuest, replace the whole folder; your settings and guides are kept.
4. Start the game. If it shows as out of date, tick "Load out of date AddOns" on the character select AddOns screen. You can check your client's interface number in game with `/dump select(4, GetBuildInfo())`.

NeverQuest's quest data comes from [QuestieDB](https://github.com/Questie/QuestieDB), the quest database the Questie team builds and keeps up to date. The CurseForge app installs it automatically with NeverQuest. If you install by hand, add the QuestieDB addon folder next to `NeverQuest` too. Questie itself is not needed. If you'd rather not use QuestieDB, choose **Build my own** in the setup wizard and NeverQuest works from what it learns in game.

## What you get

**Getting started**
- **Made to stay out of the way (1.24):** NeverQuest's own tooltips show one line by default (none on the arrow); Options > Display & Maps > Hover tooltips switches them off or to every detail. Every button and setting with choices is a dropdown, never a click that steps through values. The main window has a corner to drag to any size, and `/nq reset` puts it back.
- **Setup wizard (`/nq setup`):** a few short pages that pick the main settings: leveling style, maps and arrow, the quest tracker, automation, reminders, travel and the quest database, group play, and text size. It opens by itself the first time you log in with NeverQuest; "Use recommended" keeps the defaults and finishes in one click.
- **Help (`/nq help`, or the Help button in the main window):** an in-game guide to every feature, a full command list, key bindings and troubleshooting. `/nq help raids`, `/nq help tracker` and so on jump straight to a topic, and `/nq commands` lists every command in chat.

**Guiding**
- **Two leveling styles (Guide tab > 1-60 plan, `/nq style`, or Options > Guide & Routes):**
  - **Optimized 1-60** (default): a zone-hopping route with exact level windows, built from where current published Forever leveling guides agree. You leave a zone when its window ends, even with quests left, and some legs come back later for the leftovers when they're worth more (Westfall at 18, upper Redridge at 20, the northern Barrens at 22, Duskwood at 30, Stranglethorn at 43). Riverglades sits in the late 30s, then Tanaris, Feralas, Un'Goro, Felwood, the Plaguelands and Winterspring, with Mount Hyjal at 60.
  - **Smarter zones in Optimized (1.23, on by default; every level from 1.25):** at every level the guide reads every quest you can take in each zone that fits your level, using QuestieDB's data: the XP it gives you at your level, where its mobs, objects and drops are, and how much there is to kill or collect. Each zone is scored on its best 45 minutes of quests by XP per hour, with the trip there counted (a boat or zeppelin counts as 20 minutes, and a zone across the sea counts for 0.7 of its score so you aren't cut off from the rest of your route). The guide stays in your zone until another one is twice as good, gives your plan's zones a small edge, and falls back to the plan when fewer than four quests are known anywhere. In a simulation that levelled a human, a dwarf and a night elf from 1 to 60 on QuestieDB's Forever data, this came out about 10% faster than following the plan's level windows (around 120 hours against 133, with the same number of sea crossings). `/nq zones` lists the scores.
  - **Zone by zone (completionist, on by default):** every quest in a zone goes on the route, gray ones included, and nothing is left out for being slow. You move on when the zone is done, or once you're four levels past it, to the best zone from where you are by the same quest data (your race's plan when too little is known).
  - **It follows you (1.25):** go somewhere the plan didn't say and the guide works from there, in both styles and at any level. It stays in the zone you're in while it has quests for you, then picks the next zone from where you are. Starting zones count when they're yours, when you're standing in one, or when they're on the continent you're on (an Undead who sails to Kalimdor at level 6 gets Durotar, not a trip home). In the simulator a human who sails to Teldrassil at 5 now stays and carries on to Darkshore, 3 to 6 hours faster to 60 than being sent back to Elwynn. Untick "Zone by zone: finish every quest in a zone" under Options > Guide & Routes for the old behaviour (stay until the useful quests run out).
  - Either way, quests in the zones your style wants you in count for more in the route and the guide, quests elsewhere count for less (finished quests still get handed in when they're near), and a "Time to move on" or "Zone done" step points the arrow at the next zone at the right moment. Near the end of a leg, quests that are handed in at your next zone count for more, and follow-ups of quests you've finished count for more so chains you've started get finished.
  - The plan view shows your route leg by leg (or band by band) with your class quests, talents, riding, dual spec and dungeon windows. Browse any of the 56 race and class combinations with the Race and Class dropdowns. Levels 31-60 weren't part of Forever's beta, so those legs may get tuned.
- **Dungeon quests and their pre-quests (on by default):** for each dungeon you tick, the route picks up its quests a couple of levels before the dungeon's window, and when a quest isn't open to you yet, the first quest of the chain that opens it (for the Deadmines that's The Defias Brotherhood in Westfall). These count for a lot more than ordinary quests, and each step says what it's for. Dungeon quests in your log wait for the dungeon instead of sending you to its entrance alone. Which quests belong to which dungeon comes from QuestieDB, from the game's own Dungeon tag (that's how WoW Forever's new dungeons are covered), and from your quest log, which files dungeon quests under the dungeon's name; anything one character learns is shared by all of them. Choose dungeons in the setup wizard, with Options > Guide & Routes > Choose dungeons, or `/nq dungeons pick`; `/nq dungeons` lists them with what's waiting.
- **Route planner (Guide tab):** your objectives, turn-ins and nearby quest pickups are put into one short route so you aren't walking back and forth. Each quest is planned as "do the objective, then hand it in", using where any of your characters handed that quest in before, or where you picked it up (usually the same NPC). Turn-ins and pickups that are on the way get folded in. Quests worth too little XP for the walk are left for later, so the route keeps your XP per hour high. Finished quests handed in near each other are judged as one trip, so a pile of them in a zone you've moved on from sends you back to cash them in, and once that trip is worth making, the whole pile goes on the route rather than one quest at the end of a long walk. Each one moves up the list the further you out-level it, because quest XP shrinks as you go, and a finished quest is never dropped from the route for having gone gray: it was worth its XP when you did the work, and all that's left is collecting it. A summary row shows the trip's time, XP and XP per hour, and `/nq route` lists it in chat. The first stop stays put unless a new plan is clearly better, so the arrow doesn't flip around. Turn it off for the simple order (nearest best-XP quest first).
- **Skip, go back and the missed list (1.23):** the Skip button (or `/nq skip`, or its key binding) skips the quest the arrow is on. Quests you skip, quests you drop, and quests you leave open in zones you've moved on from all go on a missed list: Quests tab > Missed, or `/nq missed`. Each says why it's there, where it is and its level; tick Gray to include gray ones. Right-click one for Go back for it, and the route sends you there first, or take a skipped quest back. Right-click any quest in the guide or tracker for Skip, Drop and Go back for it. Turn the list off under Options > Guide & Routes.
- **Grab and drop (1.23):** pickups are valued by the quest's real XP at your level and how long its objectives take, not a flat share, so quick quests near you get grabbed and long ones far away wait. In the Optimized style the guide also points out quests in your log that aren't worth finishing: ones that have failed, gone gray, sit in a zone you've outleveled, need a group, or earn far less per hour than your route. `/nq drop` lists them, and the reminder shows in the guide once. Class, profession, dungeon and attunement quests, quests you've chosen to go back for, and chains with two or more steps to come are never suggested. Zone by zone never suggests drops.
- **Quest chains:** quests that open up follow-ups are worth more, so the guide does them sooner and grabs the follow-up while you're standing at the NPC. Chains come from the game's own quest lines and from what NeverQuest sees after your turn-ins (a quest offered right after you hand one in, by the same NPC). Steps and the tracker show "chain: Name 3/7" or "leads to 2 more quests". Learned chains are shared by all your characters.
- **Live guide (Guide tab):** always shows your best next move, in route order. When your zone runs dry or goes gray, it suggests zones in your level range. The arrow follows the top step.
- **Recorded guides (Guides tab):** record a run and replay it on alts. Steps tick off on their own, including trainer, vendor, hearth and flight master visits, and "grind to X%" steps. Export and import guides as plain text.

**Where quests are handed in**
- When a quest ends in another zone, NeverQuest asks the game which map the quest points to and reads its marker there. Without a marker it goes by, in order: where any of your characters handed it in, the NPC the quest text names (if you've met them), and the zone the text names. It only falls back to where you picked the quest up when nothing says otherwise, and it never does when the text sends you to someone other than the quest giver. Steps say "somewhere in Elwynn Forest" or "where they are isn't known yet" instead of pointing at the wrong place, and the tracker files finished quests under the zone you hand them in. Pickup and hand-in spots are only saved when you were actually at that quest's NPC (not for quests shared by a party member, started from an item, or handed in through a pop-up), and a saved spot that contradicts the quest text is ignored. Clicking a finished quest in the tracker points the arrow at its hand-in.

**Scavenger-hunt quests**
- Some quests have no objectives and no map marker: the quest text is the only clue (the Cozy Sleeping Bag chain that starts with "...and that note you found" is one). NeverQuest carries its own hand-checked spots for these, so the arrow, map and tracker point at the right object in the right zone (for Alliance, the Burned-Out Remains in The Barrens, not back in Westfall), the step says what to click and how to get there, and the chain shows as "Cozy Sleeping Bag 1/6". Text-only quests without a known spot say so instead of sending you back to where you picked them up.

**Raid attunements (Raids tab, `/nq raids`)**
- **What Forever needs:** Onyxia's Lair asks every raid member to carry the Drakefire Amulet, earned through a long quest chain that's different for each faction (16 quests for Alliance, 15 for Horde, counting The True Masters and the Tests of Skulls one by one). The Barrow Deeps and Hyjal Summit have no attunement; they're listed so you know you're set.
- **Every step:** who gives it, where, what to do, and whether you need a group or a dungeon (Blackrock Depths, Lower or Upper Blackrock Spire). Where Blizzard hasn't published something yet (minimum levels, where the four drakes for the Test of Skulls are, whether Upper Blackrock Spire needs a key in Forever), the step says so instead of guessing.
- **Ticks itself off:** a step is done when the game reports its quest finished, when you turn it in, when you're on a later quest in the chain (everything before it is done), or when the amulet is in your bags. Right-click a step to mark it done or not done yourself.
- **Follow it in the guide:** click the raid row (or the reminder the guide shows from level 50) and the chain's next step joins your guide: right after your next leveling step while you level, first once you're at the level cap. The arrow goes where the game puts the quest, or to a spot NeverQuest has learned for the NPC, and the target button targets who you need to talk to. Browse the other faction's chain with the button at the bottom.
- Settings: Options > Guide & Routes ("Raid attunements in the guide", and the level the reminder starts at).

**Class quests (Class tab, `/nq class`)**
- **In your log now:** any class quests you're on, read from the game (it files them under your class's name), with a click to point the arrow. Quests Forever has added for your class show here too.
- **What's coming:** a checklist for your class by level: what each quest gives you, where it starts and what it takes. Defensive and Berserker Stance, the totems, the demons, Bear and Aquatic Form, Redemption and Verigan's Fist, Tame Beast, Poisons, the level 50 Sunken Temple quest every class gets, and the level 60 chains. Rows you have the level for say "now"; click to tick one off.
- **Profession quests (`/nq professions`, or the Professions button in the tab):** the places a profession waits on a quest instead of a trainer: Artisan First Aid (Triage), Cooking (Clamlette Surprise) and Fishing (Nat Pagle), the armorsmith and weaponsmith paths, the three leatherworking paths, Gnomish or Goblin engineering, and smelting dark iron. Each row shows the skill it needs against yours and says "now" when you have the skill and the level. Profession quests in your log are listed at the top. Only your own professions show unless you turn that off.
- **Both are options:** Options > Guide & Routes has a switch for each section. With one off the tab keeps the other; with both off the tab is removed.
- Written for this addon from how the classes and professions work, not taken from another addon or a database. Your trainer is the authority where Forever differs.

**Spawn dots:** hover one and the first line says what it is (quest mob, drops something you need, quest object, rare or treasure) with its level when NeverQuest saw it, then which quests it belongs to, how often you've seen it there and how long ago. Mobs you've long outgrown are left off the map so an old zone isn't buried under them; anything a quest in your log points at is always shown whatever its level, and so is anything above you. The cut-off is yours to set under Options > Display & Maps.

**Maps and navigation**
- **Waypoint arrow:** rotating arrow with distance, ETA and coordinates, plus Blizzard's in-world marker. The arrow is NeverQuest's own art, drawn at 256 pixels and shown at a fraction of that with trilinear filtering and pixel snapping off, so it stays sharp at every size and angle instead of crawling as it turns. Four sizes under Options > Display & Maps. Adjustable size, compact mode. When you die and release, it points to your corpse (and says when you're close enough to resurrect), then goes back to your route once you're alive.
- **Routes, not straight lines:** when a target needs a special path, the arrow goes there first and tells you what to do ("Enter Fargodeep Mine", "Take the tunnel...", "Fly from Goldshire to Lakeshire"), with a "then:" line for the final target. It uses, in order: a flight between flight masters you've discovered, when that's clearly faster; the game's own route for quests and for map pins (caves, buildings, tunnels, zone exits); and map links to cave or building entrances. Quest distances in the guide follow the route too, so a quest at the back of a mine ranks by how far it really is. Turn either part off under Options > NeverQuest > Routes.
- **Minimap pins:** quest givers, known quests near you, objectives, turn-ins and your waypoints, small by default (Options > Scale & Text > Minimap pins: Tiny to 150%). Tiny dots show where your quests' mobs, objects and item drops are, and your own marks; other learned spots and rares stay on the world map. All of these are on by default. Your current target clings to the minimap edge when it's out of range. Works with a rotating minimap.
- **World map:** all your waypoints, numbered, plus a route preview of the guide's next steps joined by a line from where you stand (the line can be turned off or made thinner or thicker; the numbered pins stay). The route goes to objectives, finished turn-ins and pickups; where an unfinished quest will be handed in is left off the line unless you turn on "Include turn-ins for unfinished quests". When the game only marks a quest's NPC rather than its objective area, NeverQuest treats that spot as the NPC: a learned spawn spot takes over, the step says so, and the quest waits instead of sending you to the NPC early. **Alt+click** the map to drop a waypoint. Blizzard's own map pin (Ctrl+click) is picked up by the arrow too.

**Quest givers on the maps**
- **Level-appropriate pickups:** the world map shows "!" for quests you can pick up, each with its level in the quest's difficulty color (green, yellow, orange). Gray (low level) and red (too high) quests are left off unless you turn them on, and red quests also stay out of the guide and route. Dailies, story and legendary quests get their own icons.
- **Any zone:** open another zone on the world map and its quest givers show too, so you can plan where to go next. The minimap shows the ones near you, with the same filter.
- Hover a quest giver for its level, XP, who gives it (once any of your characters has taken it) and its quest chain. Click for a waypoint, Shift+click to point the arrow there.
- **Turn-in spots** ("?" for finished quests) can be added to the world map too; they're off by default because Blizzard's map usually shows them.
- Settings: Options > Display & Maps > Quest givers.

**Spawns and objectives on the maps**
- **Learned as you play:** NeverQuest ships no spawn database. It remembers where things are from your own play: where a quest mob died when you got kill credit, which corpse or object a quest item came from, where "explore" and "use" objectives ticked over, and where mobs tied to your quests came close to you (best with enemy nameplates on). Spots close together merge into one and get stronger each time.
- **Drawn for your quests:** the world map shows colored dots for the open objectives of the quests in your log (one color per quest; bigger, brighter dots have been seen more often). The minimap shows the ones near you as tiny dots (on by default; Spawns & Objectives has the switch and a dot size setting). Hover a dot to see the mob, the quest and objective it counts for, and how often and how recently it was seen. Turn on "Show every known spawn" to see all of them, with spots your quests don't need in gray.
- **Guide goes to real spawns:** when a known spot is closer than the game's quest area, the arrow goes there and the guide step says "known spot". Spots you've just visited are skipped for a few minutes, so the arrow tours the spawn points instead of parking on an empty one. The target button also learns which mob drops a quest item.
- **Mark spots yourself:** **Alt+right-click** the world map to mark a spot: your target's spawn, any open quest objective, a mob by name, or a note. `/nq mark` marks your target's spawn where you stand (or `/nq mark Hogger`), `/nq mapnote` leaves a note, and there's a key binding. Right-click a spot to set a waypoint, forget it, or remove a mark; Shift+click removes a mark.
- **Rares and treasures** the game shows on your minimap are remembered on the world map, with when you last saw them.
- **From QuestieDB, before you've been there (1.26):** with QuestieDB in use, the dots also show where QuestieDB says your open objectives' mobs are, which mobs and objects drop your quest items, and which objects to use, on the minimap near you and on the world map when zoomed in. Hover one for the mob, what it drops and the quest; it says "From QuestieDB". A finished objective's dots go, and once you've learned spots for a mob or object yourself those are drawn instead. These only draw on the maps: the arrow, the drops window and tooltips go by what you've learned, as before. "Include QuestieDB's spots for my quests" under Spawns & Objectives (or right-click one of the dots) turns them off; never with "Build my own".
- **Share:** new spots and marks go to party members who run NeverQuest. **Export** and **Import** (Options > Spawns & Objectives, or `/nq spawns export`) pass everything to friends or alts on another account as one text string.

**Nameplates and tooltips**
- **Quest icons on nameplates (`/nq plates`):** every mob your quests want gets a small icon on its nameplate: a sword to kill, a bag when it drops something you need, and a skull on the mob the guide is sending you at, with your count (3/8) underneath. They're pictures NeverQuest draws on your own screen, not raid markers: Forever no longer lets an addon place those, and nobody else sees these. What earns an icon is the game's own answer to "does this unit count for a quest I'm on" plus the drop sources NeverQuest has learned, never anything about a fight. When the game hides a unit's name from addons the icon still shows, just without the count. Left, right or above the nameplate, four sizes, and they step aside while Questie runs.
- **Mob tooltips:** a mob NeverQuest has learned drops a quest item says which quest and how far along you are (the game already lists objectives on mobs it knows about, so those aren't repeated). In a party, every mob shows how far along each member is on its quest.
- **Item tooltips:** an item an open objective is collecting says which quest wants it and your count.
- Settings: Options > Display & Maps (nameplates) and Options > NeverQuest > Tooltips.

**Quest tracker (right side)**
- **Sorted by priority, grouped by zone:** the NeverQuest tracker lists your quests in the order the guide will do them, numbered, grouped under zone headers with your current zone first. Each quest shows its level, distance, objectives, "Ready to turn in (to NPC)", its chain and party progress. Click a quest to point the arrow at it, Shift+click to link it. Right-click a quest for what other trackers offer and NeverQuest's own: point the arrow, open it in the quest log, show it on the map, link it in chat, share it with your party (always listed, greyed out when you're not in a group or the quest can't be shared), abandon it (with the game's own confirmation), pin it to the top, skip it, go back for it, or hide it. Click a zone header to fold it; right-click the tracker header for sorting (priority, distance, level) and quick options.
- **Size it by dragging (1.24):** drag the bottom corner (the one facing the middle of the screen) to set the tracker's width and the most height it grows to. It lists every quest by default and scrolls with the mouse wheel when they don't all fit, with a thin bar showing where you are. Folding it (the header or a zone) shrinks it up towards its top edge, never from the middle; a position saved by an older version is pinned by its top edge the first time it loads.
- **Blizzard's quest list:** by default it's emptied while the NeverQuest tracker shows (your quests are untracked, so Blizzard's objective tracker keeps only its other sections, like bonus objectives). Or choose "Sort by priority" to keep Blizzard's list in NeverQuest's order, or "Leave it alone". Turning the hide option off gives your tracked quests back. While the list is emptied, NeverQuest also turns off the game's automatic quest watching (`autoQuestWatch` and `autoQuestProgress`), because otherwise the game re-watches a quest the instant you accept or finish one and Blizzard's tracker flashes up before NeverQuest can untrack it again. Your own values for those two are saved and handed straight back when you stop hiding the list. Emptying the list still isn't enough on its own, because the game shows its tracker frame again regardless, so while "Hide it" is on NeverQuest also parks that frame on a hidden parent where it cannot draw, and hands it back the moment you stop hiding. Neither is ever changed during combat, since that frame holds secure quest item buttons. Turn off "Also keep Blizzard's tracker frame out of sight" under Options > Tracker if you want its bonus objectives or scenario text visible.

**Drops window (`/nq drops`)**
- **What you still have to collect:** a second, smaller window listing every quest item you're short of and everything you pick up off the ground, with how many you have of how many you need. Where the TARGET button answers "who am I looking for", this answers "what am I still short of". Mobs you only have to kill stay with the button.
- **Where it drops:** under each item, the mobs you've seen it come from and the distance to the nearest spot NeverQuest knows. These are yours, learned from the credit you've earned, not a shipped database, so a brand new item shows nothing until the first one drops for you.
- **Use it:** click an item to point the arrow at the nearest spot you know, Shift+click to link it in chat, right-click for the arrow, the link or to forget its spots. Right-click the header for sorting (closest to done, fewest left, nearest), the source line, lock and hide. Drag the header to move it.
- Off until you turn it on: `/nq drops`, the key binding, or Options > Tracker > Drops window. `/nq drops list` prints the same list in chat.

**Buttons**
- **Quest item bar:** a slim bar with the NeverQuest logo as its drag handle, holding usable quest items for your next steps (rounded buttons with cooldowns and key labels). Keybindable.
- **Target button (off by default):** targets the NPC or mob for your next step. NPC names are learned from your own quest conversations and shared by all your characters, so alts get smarter over time. Bind it under Esc > Options > Keybindings > AddOns > NeverQuest ("Target next NPC or mob"); the key works even with the button hidden. To show the "TARGET" pill on the quest item bar, turn it on under Options > Display & Maps > Buttons or type `/nq buttons target`. `/nq buttons` hides the quest item bar; its size is under Scale & Text.

**Tracking**
- **XP panel:** XP/hour, time to level, quests or kills to level, rested XP. Right-click it to open the main window.
- **Quests and Nearby tabs:** your quest log ranked by XP per distance, and quests you can pick up nearby.
- **Journal tab:** every quest you turn in, plus **level splits** that compare each level's time with your fastest character.
- **Party view:** in a party, the tracker opens with a Party section: a line for each member (class colour, how many quests you share and how many they've finished, how far away they are, and whether they run NeverQuest), "Hand in together" for quests everyone has finished, "Help" for quests they're behind on that you've finished or don't have, and "They're on" for quests they have that you lack. Click a member to point the arrow at them, click a Help line to point it at the work (right-click it for the quest menu), click the Party header to fold it. Turn it off under Options > NeverQuest > Party.
- **Works without NeverQuest:** party members who don't run NeverQuest are included. The game says which of your quests and the quests on offer near you they're on (not how far along), so the party lines, the tracker and the route still know. "See quests of members without NeverQuest" turns it off.
- **Party alerts (toggle, on by default):** a short line in your own chat, with a sound, when a party member finishes an objective of a quest you share ("Anna finished Wolf Hide (Wolf Hides)."), is ready to hand one in, or when everyone is ready to hand in together. Only you see these; what joining members send first is only remembered, so a new group doesn't flood the chat. To turn them off: right-click the tracker's Party header, type `/nq party alerts off` (or `/nq party quiet`), untick "Party alerts" in the minimap button's menu, or use Options > NeverQuest > Party. The sound has its own switch (`/nq party sound off`), and the first alert says how to turn them off.
- **Map help (toggle):** party icons on the minimap and on the world map when zoomed in mark where the work is for quests your party still needs and you can help with. Hover one for who needs it, click it to point the arrow there.
- **Party sync:** party members who also run NeverQuest see each other's progress on shared quests and which objectives each has finished.
- **Party announcements (toggle):** when you finish something, your group hears about it in party chat (instance chat in a dungeon finder group): "NeverQuest: [Quest] objective done: Boar Meat: 4/4", "NeverQuest: [Quest] complete, ready to turn in.", and raid attunement progress. Turn-ins (with the XP) can be added. Each kind has its own switch, and you can limit it to quests someone else in the group is also on, allow raid groups, drop the clickable links or the "NeverQuest:" tag. Repeats within 30 seconds are skipped, a burst of completions goes out a second apart, and nothing is sent while the game locks chat for addons. `/nq announce off` turns it all off; `/nq announce test` sends a test line. Settings: Options > NeverQuest > Party announcements.
- **Party catch-up (toggle):** in a party, the guide and route adapt so everyone keeps up. Quests you share count for more. If you've finished a quest a party member hasn't, the turn-in waits and the route takes you back to its objective spot to help ("Help Anna: ..."), then you hand it in together. Quest givers for quests a party member is on count for more, and the guide suggests quests to share (click to share) or to ask them for. Zones are picked for the lowest level in the party, and a "Regroup" step points the arrow at anyone more than 300 yards away. Levels and positions work for everyone in the party; quest lists come from members running NeverQuest. Settings: Options > NeverQuest > Party.

**Minimap button:** a round NeverQuest button on the edge of the minimap. Click to open NeverQuest, Shift+click to show or hide the tracker, right-click for quick settings (raid attunements, help, setup wizard, session summary, tracker, known quests on the world map and the minimap, XP panel, arrow, party announcements, party alerts, party catch-up, leveling style, options), and drag it anywhere around the minimap. Hide or lock it under Options > Display & Maps > Minimap, or with `/nq minimap`.

**Automation:** auto accept and turn in. **Hold Shift** when talking to an NPC to skip it.

**Reward upgrades:** with several quest rewards to choose from, each is compared with what you're wearing, using the stats your class cares about (and the armor and weapons it can use at your level). The best one gets a green arrow in the quest window and chat says why ("+4 Strength, +3 Stamina over [Old Belt]"). With no upgrade, the highest vendor value is marked. Options can take upgrades for you, or always take the highest vendor value.

**Auto repair (on by default):** opening a vendor that can repair fixes all your gear and says what it cost. It can use guild funds first when your rank allows it and the daily limit covers it. Hold Shift while opening the vendor to skip it. `/nq repair` repairs now, `/nq repair guild` switches guild funds.

**Auto loot (on by default):** takes everything from a loot window at once. Hold Shift (the game's auto-loot key) to look first. Items waiting on a group roll are left alone, and bind-on-pickup items still ask you. `/nq loot` turns it on or off.

**Sell junk (on by default):** gray items are sold when you open a vendor, and chat says what they brought in (the vendor's Buyback tab has the most recent ones if you change your mind). Hold Shift while opening the vendor to skip it. It uses the game's own sell-all-junk when the client has it, otherwise one item at a time and only while the vendor is open. `/nq junk` sells now at a vendor, or tells you what your junk is worth anywhere else.

**Quest database (`/nq db`):** every quest the game has shown any of your characters, with its level, zone and where the quest giver stands, with QuestieDB (below) filling in what it hasn't seen yet. NeverQuest's own part is built only from the game itself: a slow background scan asks the game which quests each zone offers (one zone every couple of seconds, paused in combat, again after you level), and quests you see or accept are added as you play. Alts benefit from what your other characters have seen. `/nq db` lists your zone (done, in your log, available, for later levels), `/nq db <name>` searches by name or quest ID, `/nq db zone <zone>` lists another zone, and `/nq db way <id>` points the arrow at the giver. The world map shows known quests that aren't on offer to you yet as faded markers with the level they open at.

**Learning quests you can't take yet:** the zone scan can only see what the game would offer you right now, which is why a low-level character's database used to stay nearly empty however many zones it had looked at. A quest you can see tells the game which chain it belongs to, so NeverQuest asks for the names and levels of the rest of that chain, including quests well above your level. Asks are spread out so the server isn't hammered, and paused in combat and while dead. This is how the map fills in before you have played a zone. Turn it off under Options > NeverQuest > Quest database.

**Where they show:** known quest starts draw on the world map for the whole zone and on the minimap for the ones within sight of you, both on by default (the minimap from 1.26), each with its own switch (`/nq db map`, `/nq db minimap`, or the two checkboxes under Options). They are level filtered the same way the game's own offers are: "Include gray (low level) quests" brings back ones you have outlevelled or skipped, and "Include red (too high) quests" brings in the ones your guild has sent you from zones ahead of you.

**Built from other players:** quest starts pass between NeverQuest users in your party and your guild as they are learned, so collection compounds across people instead of per character. A guild is where this pays off: people at every level across both factions know most of the world between them, and a new character inherits it over a few sessions. `/nq db ask` asks your guild outright and gets a bounded answer from a few of them. Somewhere you have been yourself is never overwritten by someone else's report; reports fill gaps and correct each other, two agreeing outweigh one who disagrees, and your own client's sighting settles it for good. `/nq db guild` and `/nq db share` turn the two channels on and off.

**Shared quest starts:** NeverQuest ships the quest starts its own scan has gathered, in `Data/Starts.lua`, so a new character sees a useful map on its first login instead of an empty one. Those starts come from NeverQuest's own scan running on other players' clients, and from party members running NeverQuest who pass on the givers they have stood in front of. Nothing in that file is taken from another addon, database project or website. Your own client always wins, field by field, so a wrong entry corrects itself the first time you walk past the real giver. `/nq db export` gives you a text string of the starts you know, to hand to a friend, to import with `/nq db import`, or to send in so it can be built into the addon for everyone; it carries quests and places only, with no character or realm names. `/nq db share` turns party sharing on and off.

**QuestieDB:** quest givers and turn-ins NeverQuest hasn't seen for itself come from [QuestieDB](https://github.com/Questie/QuestieDB), the quest database built and curated by the Questie team, GPLv3. It is its own addon, listed as a required dependency on CurseForge, so the CurseForge app installs it and keeps it up to date, and every QuestieDB update reaches you without waiting for a NeverQuest release. NeverQuest reads it through its [public API](https://github.com/Questie/QuestieDB/blob/master/docs/api.md) and ships no copy of its data. Its data stays separate from NeverQuest's own: nothing from it is written to your saved data, to `/nq db export`, or to party and guild sharing, and anything a NeverQuest client has seen in game overrides it. `/nq lib` says whether it is in use, and `/nq lib <quest id>` shows what it says about a quest and where that quest stands for this character. A QuestieDB quest only gets a marker when this character can take it now or on level alone: not another race's or class's (the new WoW Forever races included), not a profession quest without the profession and skill, not a part of a quest you aren't on, and not one behind an earlier quest you haven't done. The same requirements hold for quests NeverQuest has its own place for, so a quest one of your other characters saw doesn't show as open before it is. Dungeon quests are found through QuestieDB's own dungeon list. NeverQuest is not affiliated with Questie; thanks to the Questie team for building it.

**QuestieDB, or build your own:** the setup wizard asks where quest data should come from. **QuestieDB** (the default) fills the map from your first login. **Build my own** never reads QuestieDB, even when it is installed, and goes by NeverQuest's own data only: what your characters see, what NeverQuest ships of its own, and what your party and guild pass on. Change it any time under Options > NeverQuest > Quest database, or with `/nq db bundled on` / `off`. Without QuestieDB installed, NeverQuest says so once at login and works from its own data.

**When QuestieDB's data is wrong:** NeverQuest notices and writes it down. Stand where QuestieDB puts a quest giver and, if the game (asked again while you stand there) doesn't offer you that quest, the place is dropped for characters of your race and class, since some quests are for certain races or classes only. Take a quest or hand it in well away from where QuestieDB said, and a note keeps where it really was; what NeverQuest saw itself replaces QuestieDB's place anyway. Ctrl+click a known quest on the world map to say it isn't there yourself, which hides it for all your characters. QuestieDB itself is never changed. If the game later offers you the quest, the note settles itself. `/nq db fixes` lists the notes (`undo <id>` brings one back, `clear` forgets them all), and `/nq db export` carries them, places only, so they can be reported to the Questie team. Turn the noting off under Options if you'd rather not.

**Questie:** if you also run Questie, NeverQuest leaves quest givers and spawn spots on the maps to it so nothing is drawn twice. Your guide, route and arrow keep working. `/nq questie` switches this.

**Reminders (guide rows):** a full quest log, with which quests to drop first (failed, gray, far away or little XP for the walk; never ones ready to hand in or your attunement chain); low food and water, or a hunter's ammo; nearly full bags, with quest items you can safely delete because their quest is done. Logging out away from an inn or city reminds you there's no rested XP there (and mentions your Cozy Sleeping Bag if you carry one). Right-click any reminder row (these, flight paths, hearth) to hide it until you log in again.

**Flight paths and hearthstone:** a guide step points to any flight master in your zone you haven't talked to yet. NeverQuest notes where you bind your hearthstone; when it's ready, nothing is left near you, and finished quests are handed in near your inn, the guide's first step says to hearth back and the hearthstone appears on the quest item bar. At an inn far from your hearth spot with quests around, it says once that you could bind there.

**Session summary (`/nq session`):** time played, XP and XP per hour, levels, quests, kills, gold and deaths since you logged in. Optionally shown when you log out.

**Sharing as you go:** with "Share quests with my party as I pick them up" on (Options > NeverQuest > Party, off by default), each quest you take from an NPC is offered to your party. A quest that was shared to you isn't shared back, and nothing is sent in a raid.

**Finished quests are never left behind:** a quest you've completed is earned XP, so it's never filed under "later". When NeverQuest hasn't learned where one is handed in it can't put it on the route, but a notice at the top of the guide counts how many are waiting in the zone you're standing in, and they're listed above anything that takes you out of the zone, so the route never sends you out of a town past three turn-ins. They stay below work that's in front of you, and because the only spot they carry is usually the middle of a zone, the arrow never follows one on its own; clicking the row still points it there.

**Profession nearly capped:** a reminder when a profession is within ten points of its current rank, read from the cap the game reports rather than a table of thresholds. In a town it says to train the next rank there; out in the world it says to do it on your next trip in.

**Upgrades and junk in your bags:** the guide adds a row when something in your bags beats what you're wearing, judged the same way as quest rewards, with what it replaces and the stat difference. When your bags are nearly full it also names the cheapest piece of junk, the one to throw out if you need a slot before the next vendor.

**Flight times:** the game doesn't tell an addon how long a flight takes, so NeverQuest times each planned flight the first time you take it and counts it down on the arrow from then on.

**Changing your mind:** a flight plan is dropped as soon as you leave the place it was sending you out from, whether you took a different flight, hearthed, used a portal or took a boat. The route is then worked out again from wherever you actually are.

**Asking for a group:** Ctrl-click an elite or group quest in the guide, or pick "Ask for a group" in its tracker menu, to put an LFG message with the quest link in your chat box. Nothing is sent until you press Enter. `/nq lfg` does it for your next elite quest.

## Settings

Esc > Options > AddOns > NeverQuest has six pages, and almost everything can be switched on or off:

- **NeverQuest:** automation (quests, reward choice, auto loot, sell junk, repairs and guild funds), reminders (full quest log, supplies, bags, rested XP, session summary), travel (flight paths, hearthstone, home inn), the quest database (background scan, world map markers, Questie), alerts, party sharing, party announcements (objectives, quests, turn-ins, attunements, shared quests only, raids, links, tag) party catch-up (group level, regroup, share suggestions) and the party view, quests of members without NeverQuest, party alerts (with a sound) and map help, chat messages.
- **Guide & Routes:** leveling style (optimized 1-60 or zone by zone), choosing zones from the quest data, drop suggestions, completionist zone by zone, the missed list, raid attunement steps and reminder level, following your 1-60 plan, move-on reminders, route planning, quest chains, pickups along the way, "then turn in" steps, the route summary, zone and plan advice, rested and outleveled tips, what each step shows (XP, directions, party), paths and flights.
- **Tracker:** show it, what happens to Blizzard's list, sort order, group by zone, current zone first, collapse other zones, finished quests first, gray quests, each line it shows (objectives, finished objectives, numbers, level, distance, chain, party), how many quests, width, height, background and size.
- **Display & Maps:** the minimap button, each XP panel line, the arrow's lines (coordinates, ETA, "then:"), arrival sound and distance, quest buttons, minimap and world map pins, and quest givers (world map, other zones, minimap, levels, gray, red, dailies, turn-in spots).
- **Spawns & Objectives:** spawn learning, maps, sharing and your spawn data.
- **Scale & Text:** font, text size (Small to Huge), outline and shadow for every NeverQuest panel, plus separate sizes for the main window, tracker, XP panel, arrow, quest buttons, minimap pins and world map pins.

## Making guides for every race

The live guide works for any race from level 1. For a curated route:

1. On the character, `/nq record Dwarf Hunter 1-12` and play normally.
2. Pickups, completions, turn-ins (with NPC names), flights, hearths, trainers, vendors and level-ups are saved with coordinates. Use `/nq grind` after grinding and `/nq note` for tips.
3. `/nq stop`. The guide appears in the Guides tab on all your characters.
4. **Export** gives you text to share. **Import** loads someone else's. Guides from version 1.0 still import.

## Notes

- Forever runs addons under the Midnight rules, so nothing here reads combat data or makes combat decisions. Buttons only change outside combat; changes made during a fight apply when it ends.
- The plan matches zones by their English names. On other game languages the plan still shows, but zone navigation from it may not work; `/nq check` reports how many plan zones it found.
- Party sync, the party view, alerts and map help work in parties, not raids, and sync pauses in content where the game blocks addon messages. For members without NeverQuest the game only says whether they're on a quest, not how far along.
- Spawn learning stays off inside dungeons and on flights. Nearby-mob learning only checks range outside combat (the game blocks that check in a fight), so kill credit and loot do the work during fights. Spot positions are where you stood, so ranged kills land up to about 40 yards from the mob.
- Routes to `/way` waypoints use the in-world waypoint marker setting (on by default), because the game only plans a route for the pin it's tracking.
- Quest difficulty comes from the game's own rating for your level. Blizzard's map shows its own "!" too; NeverQuest's sit on top with the level added.
- The route planner uses straight-line distances with an allowance for real paths, and guesses time per objective from its type and count. Its XP per hour is an estimate for comparing routes, not a promise.
- Turn-in spots for quests you're still doing are guesses until one of your characters has handed that quest in; the first time through, the pickup spot stands in.
- Raid attunement steps follow Blizzard's raid overview (November 2026); NPCs and places come from published guides, and some quest IDs come from original Classic. Raid details may change before or after the raids open on December 9. If a step ticks off wrongly, right-click it.
- Party announcements use the game's normal chat (party or instance chat); addons can't post there while the game locks chat for them, so those messages are skipped rather than sent late.
- A two-handed reward is compared with your main hand and off-hand together, and no reward is judged or taken while the game is still loading its stats.
- Reward upgrades use simple stat weights per class (the main stats, armor and weapon damage). They're good at spotting a clear upgrade, not a replacement for a gear planner, and the weights don't know your talents.
- NeverQuest's own quest database knows what the game has shown one of your characters, what your party has passed on, and the quest starts shipped in `Data/Starts.lua`, so it fills in as you and your alts play. All of it was gathered from the game itself by NeverQuest; nothing in it is copied from other addons or websites. QuestieDB, when it's in use, fills in the rest without being copied into it. Class and race quests seen by another character can show on the map until this character's scan of that zone confirms they aren't for it.
- Text matching for reminders (food and drink detection by spell name, the Cozy Sleeping Bag) is in English.
- Saved settings: `NeverQuestDB` (account) and `NeverQuestCharDB` (per character).
- If a feature hits repeated errors, NeverQuest pauses just that feature and tells you; `/reload` brings it back. `/nq report` collects the details.
- Testing a new build? Follow TESTING.md.
